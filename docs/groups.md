# Groups and calendar sharing

An administrator with recent primary authentication and passkey verification manages local groups under **Administration → Sharing groups**. Groups have stable internal identifiers, a display name and up to 1,000 account members. The interface creates, renames, replaces membership and deletes groups. Deleting a group removes its collection grants. Account disabling still denies access even when its membership remains recorded.

A collection owner uses **Group sharing** on the account page to grant read-only or read/write access, or remove a grant. Sharing references the same authoritative collection. Administrators manage membership: granting a group therefore delegates control of its current and future membership. An administrator explicitly adding their own account receives that group's granted access; administrative status by itself does not bypass calendar permissions. Individual account grants and group grants combine to provide the effective access.

The sharing dropdown exposes group names and identifiers. DAV membership metadata is limited to members, owners of collections granted to the group, and administrators. Group administration, owned grants and effective calendar permissions are checked in the services as well as the HTTP layer. Mutations require current account state and recent MFA; browser forms retain CSRF protection.

Membership/grant changes reconcile shared collection bindings and change affected collection synchronization generations. A client with an invalidated stable or continuation token must perform an initial synchronization again; continuation state cannot retain access after account disabling or permission revocation. Removing group access preserves any access separately granted to the same account. Backup/restoration retains groups, grants, memberships and retired principal identifiers; operators must review these restored permissions before re-enabling accounts.

The directory supports at most 1,000 groups, at most 1,000 accounts per group and 10,000 recorded group memberships in total. Group creation/replacement checks these existing admission bounds before accepting changes. No separate grant-count ceiling makes accepted sharing states impossible to maintain: a group may be granted many collections and an owner may have more than 10,000 group grants.

The account dashboard lists owned group grants in pages of 50, with Previous/Next controls and removal actions on every page. Membership maintenance streams affected collection locks in stable UUID order; V13 reconciles bindings and generations with set-based SQL under the exclusive mutation lock and one transaction. Readers therefore observe the complete permission change after commit, rather than partially revoked batches. Group deletion removes its memberships and grants atomically. Individual READ/WRITE grants continue to contribute independently to effective access. Group/principal retirement identifiers remain durable and are never reused.

`GroupServiceIT` exercises many grants across a modest number of calendars/groups and a single group spanning more than 10,000 calendars, including listing, removal, membership replacement and deletion. The required packaged workload additionally seeds 12,500 disposable grants across 25 calendars and 500 groups, then uses actual CSRF-protected browser forms to inspect the first/last pages, revoke a grant, replace membership and delete a group while recording Docker/DB observations. SQL fixture population is not a measurement of HTTP grant-creation throughput.
